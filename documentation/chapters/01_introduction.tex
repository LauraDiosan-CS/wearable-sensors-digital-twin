\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chapter:introduction}

Cardiovascular diseases are the leading cause of death worldwide, with an estimated 19.8 million deaths in 2022 \cite{who2025cvd}. Smartwatches can already screen people for heart-rhythm problems, but a single-lead wearable ECG is noisy and limited on its own \cite{bayoumy2021smart}. Digital twins, models of one patient that are updated with that patient's data, are a promising way to personalise monitoring \cite{corralacero2020digital}. However, current cardiac twins are mostly built from hospital data such as medical imaging \cite{gillette2021framework}.

We build a simple twin from wearable data alone: a heartbeat model \cite{mcsharry2003dynamical} fitted to the wearer's own beats, combined with machine-learning models that clean the signal, detect arrhythmias and forecast heart rate. Five agents share the twin and write a report for a clinician. The key idea is to measure how far each new beat is from the patient's own normal.
