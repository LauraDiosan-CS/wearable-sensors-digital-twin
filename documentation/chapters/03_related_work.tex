\chapter{State of the art/Related work}
\label{chapter:stateOfArt}

We searched Crossref, Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, PubMed and the web for work in the areas below.

\section{Cardiac digital twins}
\label{sec:rw-twins}

Corral-Acero et al.~\cite{corralacero2020digital} define a healthcare digital twin as a model that combines a person's data over time, with wearables as one of its data sources. They argue that combining physiological models with statistical models learned from data is the way towards precision cardiology.

Most implemented cardiac twins use hospital data. Gillette et al.~\cite{gillette2021framework} build the heart's anatomy from MR images in a few hours per patient and fit its electrical behaviour to a 12-lead ECG, which they demonstrated for a single subject. Simonian et al.~\cite{simonian2025development} build a twin of the mitral valve that integrates the patient's follow-up data to predict the valve's state after a repair procedure. Thangaraj et al.~\cite{thangaraj2024cardiovascular} describe hybrid twins that combine physiological models with lightweight machine learning. They see smartwatches as a way to close the loop with the patient, but note that this only works if people keep wearing them. Closest to our work, Roop et al.~\cite{roop2025tuning} model the heart's electrical conduction system and personalise it with wearable signals, so that it produces ECGs that match the individual.

\paragraph{Difference.} Our twin needs only a wearable, and it is used for continuous monitoring rather than simulation.

\section{Wearable ECG and signal quality}
\label{sec:rw-wearables}

Bayoumy et al.~\cite{bayoumy2021smart} review wearables in cardiovascular care. They find that a single-lead ECG is enough only for the simpler arrhythmias, and list accuracy, privacy, cost and separating useful information from noise as the main barriers to adoption. Bhavsar et al.~\cite{bhavsar2021digital} treat wearable measurements as digital biomarkers and propose a platform that connects patients and doctors and adds insights from continuous monitoring to clinical reports. Del-Valle-Soto et al.~\cite{delvallesoto2024unveiling} survey wearable health applications, describe how they enable real-time feedback and personalised interventions, and stress the importance of privacy and data security.

Noise is the main technical obstacle. Sameni et al.~\cite{sameni2007nonlinear} place a mathematical model of the ECG, the same kind of model our twin is based on, inside a Kalman filter that follows the signal over time. This removed noise better than standard filters over a wide range of noise levels.

\paragraph{Difference.} We judge noise removal by how much it helps arrhythmia detection, not on its own.

\section{Arrhythmia classification}
\label{sec:rw-classification}

De Chazal et al.~\cite{dechazal2004automatic} split the MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database into two groups of patients, one for training and one for testing, which became the reference way to evaluate beat classifiers. With this split, their classifier detected some beat types, especially supraventricular beats, poorly. Kiranyaz et al.~\cite{kiranyaz2016realtime} therefore train a small network for each patient, using common data plus the patient's own labelled beats. It is fast enough for real-time monitoring and, according to the authors, could run on a lightweight wearable device. Hannun et al.~\cite{hannun2019cardiologist} trained a deep network on single-lead ambulatory ECG that recognises 12 rhythm classes as well as cardiologists. They see its role in triage, with results confirmed by a clinician.

\paragraph{Difference.} Instead of training a model per patient, we give a general model the twin's deviation score, which needs no labelled data from the new patient.

\section{Heart models and forecasting}
\label{sec:rw-models}

McSharry et al.~\cite{mcsharry2003dynamical} proposed the heartbeat model behind our twin, originally to generate realistic synthetic ECG for testing signal-processing methods. The model is controlled by a few intuitive settings: the heart rate, how much it varies, and the shape of the P, Q, R, S and T waves.

For forecasting heart rate, the Temporal Fusion Transformer \cite{lim2021temporal} predicts several steps ahead and shows which inputs matter most. It combines different kinds of input, such as fixed information and past measurements, and was more accurate than earlier methods on real-world datasets.

\paragraph{Difference.} We use the heartbeat model as a personal reference for each patient, not to generate data.

\section{AI agents in healthcare}
\label{sec:rw-agents}

PHIA \cite{merrill2026transforming} is a language-model agent that analyses wearable health data by writing code and looking up information. In an evaluation by experts, it answered 84\% of factual numerical questions correctly, showing that agents can reason over personal wearable data when they use tools instead of guessing.

\paragraph{Difference.} Our agents coordinate the models and write the report; the clinician makes the decisions.
